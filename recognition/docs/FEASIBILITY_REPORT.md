# Feasibility Report — HipMRI 3D Multi-Class Segmentation

## 1. Executive Summary

This project investigates six-class segmentation of three-dimensional HipMRI scans for research relevant to prostate radiotherapy planning. It compares a Standard 3D U-Net with an Improved 3D U-Net that adds volumetric residual units and deep supervision. The dataset, patient-level partition, preprocessing, training, checkpointing and full-volume Validation experiments have progressed beyond the original proposal. These completed stages establish that a controlled research comparison is technically executable on the available UQ Rangpur resources. They do not establish dependable prostate contours, performance on held-out patients or suitability for clinical use. The project remains feasible as a research and course project, subject to a verified final inference procedure, a formal model decision, held-out evaluation and assessment deliverables.

## 2. Problem Definition and User Needs

Radiotherapy planning depends on identifying a target region and nearby organs at risk. Under-contouring a target could omit tissue intended for treatment; over-contouring could increase exposure of healthy tissue. Errors in delineating adjacent structures such as the bladder and rectum can likewise affect planning decisions. These consequences motivate investigation of segmentation assistance, but a voxel-overlap score alone cannot certify a clinically acceptable boundary or dose plan. A defensible research goal is to produce candidate three-dimensional contours for specialist inspection and correction, while documenting where the models fail. No present result supports autonomous contouring or clinical deployment.

The scientific question is whether an Improved volumetric network yields useful segmentation gains over a conventional baseline under a common data and evaluation protocol, and what limitations remain across anatomical classes and patients. This includes examining false positives, false negatives and individual failures rather than relying on one aggregate score. The [Final Report](../FINAL-REPORT.md) contains the detailed Validation comparisons and case studies; this report assesses whether the investigation can be carried out and interpreted responsibly.

## 3. Proposed Technical Approach

The Standard 3D U-Net supplies an established encoder–decoder baseline with skip connections that combine broad context with spatial detail. Three-dimensional convolutions use neighbouring slices directly, which is appropriate for volumetric anatomy, although their memory cost constrains the field of view. The Improved 3D U-Net retains this volumetric design and adds residual paths to support optimisation through deeper feature transformations. Auxiliary supervision at decoder scales supplies training signals to intermediate representations. Both changes are technically motivated, but their separate causal contributions have not been isolated in this project.

The comparison holds the patient split, preprocessing, patch policy and full-volume evaluator in common. The Improved V1–V4 labels denote successive research and optimisation experiments using the same `ImprovedUNet3D` architecture, rather than four different network designs. V2 changed the training loss to address minority classes; V3 continued the selected V2 state; V4 tested a controlled change to the Prostate overlap penalty. These choices make the sequence useful for studying optimisation and trade-offs while preserving the Standard model as the architectural reference. The [experiment history](experiments.md) records the specific protocols and evidence limits.

## 4. Data and Computational Feasibility

Production discovery found 211 matched MRI–segmentation volumes from 38 patients, with labels for Background, Body, Bones, Bladder, Rectum and Prostate. The fixed split assigns 143 volumes from 26 patients to Train, 38 from six patients to Validation and 30 from six patients to held-out Test. All longitudinal scans from a patient remain in one partition, preventing direct patient overlap. A header audit found matching MRI and mask grids; it does not itself establish annotation quality. Real-data loading and preprocessing were subsequently checked on the Train and Validation volumes. The [implementation README](../hipmri_3d_improved_unet_hard/README.md) documents those checks and their scope.

The implemented pipeline validates image–mask geometry, resamples both to a shared physical grid with interpolation appropriate to continuous MRI and discrete labels, and normalises MRI intensity without using mask values to calculate its statistics. Its spacing policy derives from Train data. Identical crops support patch-based 3D learning within GPU memory, while overlapping sliding windows aggregate full-volume predictions at Validation. These measures address practical scale and alignment, although patch context and resampling may still remove useful boundary information. Integrity checks, explicit patient assignments, recorded configurations and checkpoints support reproducibility; the availability of every historical checkpoint payload and a final compatible inference path remain distinct questions.

Training and Validation have run on UQ Rangpur NVIDIA A100 resources. The completed runs and selected-case inference records show that the resource demands are manageable for the experiments already performed. They do not constitute a repeated, matched benchmark of total training cost or guarantee future allocation. Memory-aware loading, patch training, tiled inference and resumable checkpoints provide practical controls if runtime or memory becomes restrictive. Additional experiments would require their own resource estimates and controlled records.

## 5. Preliminary Findings and Feasibility Evidence

Both Standard and Improved implementations completed their original training and full-volume Validation evaluations. Early comparisons showed better overlap for some larger classes with the Improved network, yet severe omission of minority structures, including Prostate and Rectum, persisted. Inspected paired failures include a patient-specific Bladder regression as well as gains. The [class-collapse analysis](analysis/class-prediction-collapse.md) and [failure-case evidence](evidence/failure-cases.md) therefore support a limited conclusion: the models and evaluator operate on real data, but aggregate improvements do not imply reliable contouring of the primary organ.

Subsequent weighted-loss and continuation experiments investigated these failures under controlled changes and yielded nonzero minority-class segmentation in the recorded Validation results. V3 E12 was selected and remains the protected candidate. This is evidence that the failure mode can be investigated and partially addressed through optimisation, not proof that class weighting or extra epochs automatically solves it. Different losses also change error balances, and the available experiments cannot establish a single cause of the earlier omissions.

The V4 E20 paired Control/Treatment Validation completed under predeclared safeguards. Treatment reduced *pooled* Prostate false positives, but its Prostate *patient-macro* Dice gain, pooled recall loss and another class's patient-macro regression failed mandatory acceptance conditions. Treatment was rejected, Control E20 was not automatically selected, and V3 E12 remained protected. This is a useful feasibility result: the pipeline can measure and enforce a trade-off before selecting a model. It does not establish that fewer Prostate false positives amount to overall improvement. The independently audited decision is preserved in the local-only research archive; the current [experiment history](experiments.md) summarises it for repository readers. Because the same six Validation patients informed iterative development, these findings are exploratory and cannot measure independent generalisation.

## 6. Technical Risks and Mitigation Strategies

**Class imbalance and difficult boundaries.** Small anatomical classes compete with dominant Background and Body voxels, and limited MRI tissue contrast can obscure boundaries. Foreground-aware sampling and weighted loss have been implemented, with partial improvement and residual errors. Future sampling, loss or resolution changes require separate, controlled evaluation; no one change guarantees a clinically meaningful contour.

**Competing error types and spatial context.** Penalising false positives can increase false negatives or worsen another organ, as V4 demonstrated. The existing acceptance rule protects recall and other classes, while per-patient review and paired failure cases expose harm hidden by pooled results. Patch size, spacing and sliding-window inference balance three-dimensional context against memory; possible changes to these settings remain proposals until tested against the common protocol.

**Selection bias, compute and reproducibility.** Repeated use of six Validation patients can overfit model choices to that cohort. Patient-disjoint Test data have been preserved, but only a frozen model and verified inference procedure can make their evaluation interpretable. Rangpur access, checkpoint compatibility and incomplete historical payload inspection are operational risks. Existing configuration records, checkpoint identities, resumable training and independent audits reduce those risks; they do not replace a final end-to-end compatibility check. More GPU time would not, by itself, resolve scientific uncertainty.

## 7. Remaining Requirements and Overall Feasibility Assessment

The dataset split, shared preprocessing, both model families, original Validation comparisons and the controlled V3/V4 decisions have been demonstrated to the evidence levels described above. The protected V3 E12 candidate has **not** undergone a formal final-model freeze. The current `predict.py` has **not** been independently validated as its final inference path; the local-only P4-05A readiness audit identifies this compatibility gap. A separate [V3 adapter](../hipmri_3d_improved_unet_hard/predict_v3.py) now implements protected candidate checks, but only synthetic CPU admission has been verified. Real checkpoint/receipt inspection and Validation equivalence remain pending. A verified inference path and freeze decision must precede the untouched held-out Test evaluation. Test performance, clinical accuracy, deployment suitability and final assessment completion are consequently unknown. Course-facing deliverables must be checked against the applicable official assessment specification; this repository's documentation is supporting evidence, not a substitute for that specification.

Overall, the project is technically feasible and scientifically meaningful as a bounded research prototype: it has already supported real volumetric experiments and disciplined rejection of an unfavourable treatment. Completion as a defensible course project depends on closing the inference and provenance gaps, preserving the independent Test boundary, reporting class- and patient-level limitations honestly, and meeting the official submission requirements. Even successful Test evaluation would require separate clinical validation before any use in patient care.
